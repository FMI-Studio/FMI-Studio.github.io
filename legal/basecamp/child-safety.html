Child Safety Standards

Effective date: November 21, 2026 · Last updated: November 23, 2026

FMI Studio has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation. These standards apply to BaseCamp and prohibit any use of the service to facilitate, promote, or engage in child sexual abuse or exploitation.

1. Scope and age requirement

BaseCamp is intended for adults aged 18 or older and is not intended for children. Users must not create or use a BaseCamp account if they are under 18. This age restriction does not reduce our commitment to preventing child sexual abuse and exploitation, including conduct that targets or harms children outside the service.

2. Prohibited conduct

BaseCamp strictly prohibits:

  • Child sexual abuse or exploitation in any form.
  • Grooming, solicitation, coercion, trafficking, or attempts to arrange sexual contact with a child.
  • Creating, requesting, possessing, sharing, distributing, or promoting child sexual abuse material (CSAM).
  • Using groups, presence information, invitations, or location-related features to identify, approach, monitor, intimidate, exploit, or endanger a child.
  • Threats, blackmail, or other abusive conduct involving a child.
  • Attempting to evade enforcement or helping another person engage in prohibited conduct.

3. How to report a child-safety concern

If you believe a child may be at risk or that BaseCamp is being used for child sexual abuse or exploitation, use the Laporkan Masalah (Report a Problem) form in the BaseCamp app. Select the relevant report category, describe the incident, and submit the report. You can also contact FMI Studio directly at fmi.colaboration@gmail.com.

Please include, where available:

  • An email address for follow-up correspondence, entered in the form. The address is not independently verified.
  • The reported Account ID and related Group ID, if known (both optional fields).
  • A factual description of what happened, when it happened, and why you believe a child may be at risk.

You do not need to know the child's identity to raise a concern. Do not attach, forward, download, or otherwise send CSAM or other sexual imagery of a child. Do not put a child at further risk to obtain evidence. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or the appropriate child-protection authority.

In-app reporting is available: After a report is submitted successfully, BaseCamp displays a confirmation and a unique report reference number. The report is sent through the BaseCamp backend to FMI Studio’s designated reporting inbox for review. Include the reference number in any follow-up correspondence. Reports are not automatically verified merely because they have been submitted.

4. Review and action

FMI Studio first reviews the information submitted and assesses the nature and urgency of the concern. When details are insufficient, our normal process is to contact the reporter using the correspondence email provided, request clarification, and evaluate any additional information received. The correspondence email is not independently verified, and a response is not guaranteed. A report alone does not establish that the reported person is responsible.

Urgent exception: Where the initial report contains credible evidence of serious child-safety harm, suspected CSAM, or an immediate risk to a child, FMI Studio may take protective action and contact competent authorities without waiting for correspondence. We do not require a reporter to establish criminal guilt before raising a serious concern with the appropriate authority.

Depending on the circumstances and available information, actions may include:

  • Requesting clarification from the reported account.
  • Issuing a formal warning.
  • Temporarily suspending an account while a concern is reviewed or as an enforcement measure.
  • Permanently banning an account.
  • Closing a case when a concern is not substantiated or has been addressed.
  • Escalating a serious matter to the appropriate authority when required by law or warranted by the circumstances.

We do not automatically suspend or ban an account solely because a report was submitted. We may take urgent protective action where reasonably necessary to address an immediate safety risk. We record relevant case decisions and actions, subject to applicable retention and privacy requirements.

5. Cooperation with authorities

Reports submitted through BaseCamp are not automatically forwarded to government agencies or law enforcement. FMI Studio reviews reports and, where appropriate, may assist with referrals to competent authorities in Indonesia, including the Ministry of Women’s Empowerment and Child Protection’s SAPA 129 service or the police. Reporters may also contact those authorities directly without using BaseCamp.

FMI Studio will comply with applicable legal reporting obligations, including reporting confirmed CSAM to the appropriate authority as required. Where legally required or necessary to address a serious child-safety risk, we may preserve and disclose relevant information to competent authorities through appropriate channels. We document moderation decisions and any referrals, using the report reference number where available. We do not ask reporters to send CSAM through ordinary email or messaging channels.

6. Privacy and sensitive information

Reports are handled as confidential safety information and access is restricted to people who need it to review and address the concern. We use the information provided for safety review, communication with the reporter where possible, enforcement, and lawful escalation. We do not promise absolute confidentiality where disclosure is required by law or necessary to address a serious risk.

Do not send passwords, authentication tokens, identity documents, or CSAM in a report. Our general data practices are described in the BaseCamp Privacy Policy.

7. Contact

For child-safety concerns or questions about these standards, contact FMI Studio at fmi.colaboration@gmail.com.
